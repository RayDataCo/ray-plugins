# Debt Schedule

One job: given a debt instrument's terms — or that instrument's data plus covenant inputs, or a refinance/prepayment offer — produce the instrument-level debt mechanics: amortization schedule, floating-rate interest, covenant compliance, refinance evaluation. Single instrument (or a small named set), on its own contractual terms — not a portfolio, not a project-investment call, not a whole-org cash forecast.

## Procedure

**1. Confirm scope, instrument identity, decline the three neighbors.** Identify which sub-capability(ies) are wanted: amortization schedule, floating-rate interest, covenant test, refinance/prepayment evaluation, or a combination. Confirm this concerns a single instrument (or a small named set) on its own contractual terms. If the ask is actually a portfolio-wide liquidity question, a project-investment worthiness call, or a whole-organization cash forecast — STOP, decline, and redirect to treasury-liquidity-analysis, capital-budgeting-analysis, or cash-flow-forecasting respectively (see "When NOT to use" below). State which sub-capability(ies) you will compute before computing them.

**2. Inventory the four core attributes; never assume the day-count convention.** Collect: principal (current outstanding balance, not just original face), rate (fixed, or floating with reference rate + spread + reset frequency + day-count convention), term, and amortization structure (level-payment, level-principal, bullet, balloon, revolver, or hybrid — see `references/structures.md`). Name any missing attribute rather than assuming it. Above all: confirm the day-count convention (ACT/360 vs. 30/360) from the governing document. ACT/360 dominates USD SOFR loans; 30/360 is common in fixed-rate bond/loan docs; the two produce different, individually plausible-looking answers. If it isn't stated, flag it as unconfirmed and ask — never default to "probably ACT/360."

**3. Build the amortization schedule (level-payment case).** Periodic rate `r = annual rate / periods per year`, total periods `n`. `PMT = P·r / (1 − (1+r)^−n)`. Per row: `Interest = Beginning balance × r` (rounded to the cent), `Principal = PMT − Interest`, `Ending balance = Beginning balance − Principal`. Show every row's arithmetic, not just the finished table. Confirm interest declines and principal rises monotonically period over period as a sanity check. If the instrument is level-principal, bullet, balloon, or a revolver instead, apply that structure's mechanics (`references/structures.md`) — never force the level-payment formula onto a non-level-payment instrument.

**4. Cross-check the balance two independent ways.** For at least one requested period, verify the iterative ending balance against the closed-form formula `Balance(k) = P(1+r)^k − PMT × [((1+r)^k − 1)/r]`. A small cent-scale drift between the two is expected and explainable — per-period cent-rounding of interest means slightly less principal is applied each period than the continuous formula assumes — state it as such, never as an error to chase down. Total interest over the life = `PMT × n − P`.

**5. Compute floating-rate interest using the confirmed day-count convention.** `All-in rate = Reference rate (that period's reset) + Spread`. ACT/360: `Interest = Principal × All-in rate × (actual calendar days / 360)`. 30/360: `Interest = Principal × All-in rate × (30-day-count days in the period / 360)` — a standard 30/360 quarter is exactly 90/360 = 0.25 regardless of the actual calendar. Never substitute "annual rate ÷ 4" for a quarterly ACT/360 instrument — that shortcut is mathematically identical to always assuming a 90/360 quarter, and silently understates interest in every quarter that isn't exactly 90 actual days (most of them). When a cross-convention comparison is useful or requested, compute both and quantify the dollar and percentage difference explicitly.

**6. Test covenant compliance against the SPECIFIC agreement's own definitions.** Pull CFADS and Debt Service (for DSCR), or Total Debt and EBITDA (for leverage), from the specific credit agreement given — never a generic textbook formula; CFADS is not standardized and varies by agreement (maintenance-vs-total capex deductions, reserve/sinking-fund treatment, whether mandatory prepayments count as Debt Service — see `references/covenants.md`). `DSCR = CFADS / Debt Service` vs. the stated minimum; `Leverage = Total Debt / EBITDA` vs. the stated maximum. State PASS/FAIL. Quantify headroom in BOTH directions per covenant — e.g. for DSCR: the CFADS cushion holding Debt Service constant (`Actual CFADS − minimum required CFADS`), AND the Debt-Service cushion holding CFADS constant (`maximum supportable Debt Service − actual Debt Service`) — different dollar figures, both valid. Flag that comparing raw-dollar headroom across covenants of different base size, without the ratio-cushion percentage, can make a tighter covenant look more comfortable than it is.

**7. Evaluate refinance/prepayment on an all-in-cost basis, including the EXISTING loan's own exit cost.** State the naive rate-only comparison as an explicit strawman first. Then: total upfront cost (new loan) = origination fee + closing costs + any new-loan prepayment penalty/breakage/make-whole. Annual savings = `Principal × rate differential`. Breakeven (years) = `Total upfront cost / Annual savings`, compared against the remaining term on the existing loan. Net savings over the full remaining life = `(Annual savings × remaining years) − total upfront cost`. Then apply the judgment layer: explicitly ask whether the EXISTING loan being retired carries its own prepayment penalty, breakage cost (if hedged with a swap), or make-whole provision (`references/refinance-judgment.md`) — these live in the existing loan's own documentation, not the new offer's term sheet, and are easy to omit because nobody hands them to the analyst unprompted. If not given, flag this as an open, unconfirmed question rather than silently assuming zero — especially when the computed net margin is thin, since even a modest unconfirmed exit cost could flip the decision.

**8. Emit the deliverable.** In order: scope line → instrument summary (day-count convention marked confirmed-from-document or flagged-as-assumed) → amortization table with the cross-check → floating-rate calculation → covenant results with dual-direction headroom → refinance evaluation with the existing-loan exit-cost flag → caveats (any assumed or missing inputs, stated explicitly). Always show the formula and the actual numbers plugged in per line, not just the answer.

## Output contract

- PMT via the exact annuity formula, never approximated; every amortization row shows visible arithmetic; interest declines / principal rises monotonically period over period.
- Amortization balance cross-checked against the closed-form formula for at least one period; any cent-scale drift is explicitly explained as per-period rounding, never silently ignored or misreported as an error.
- Day-count convention is always stated as confirmed-from-document or flagged-as-assumed; ACT/360 interest is never approximated via the naive annual-rate-÷-4 / assumed-90-day-quarter shortcut.
- DSCR and leverage use the specific agreement's own stated definitions, never a generic formula substituted without confirmation; headroom is quantified in both directions for every covenant tested.
- Any refinance/prepayment evaluation nets ALL new-loan upfront costs against savings on a breakeven basis, AND explicitly states whether the existing loan's own exit cost is included, confirmed absent, or an open unconfirmed question — never silently assumed to be zero.
- Rounding discipline is stated and applied consistently: cent-level rounding per period is standard; a fully-trued schedule's final payment may be adjusted by a few cents to zero the balance exactly at maturity.

## When NOT to use

- **treasury-liquidity-analysis** — the portfolio-wide ratio/liquidity-structure view across all facilities and cash ("what's our current ratio," "do we have enough liquidity across the whole balance sheet"). This skill supplies one instrument's figures (a balance, a debt-service amount); it does not aggregate across the whole balance sheet.
- **capital-budgeting-analysis** — whether a project or the decision to finance it is worth undertaking ("what's the NPV/IRR/payback of this investment"). This skill tracks a debt instrument's mechanics once the financing decision has already been made.
- **cash-flow-forecasting** — the forward-looking, whole-organization weekly cash view ("what will our cash position be next quarter," "build our 13-week forecast"). This skill's debt-service output is one precisely-known input that forecast consumes; it does not itself forecast the organization's full cash position.
- A request with no instrument terms at all ("explain how amortization works," with no principal/rate/term/amortization type given) — nothing to compute; ask for concrete instrument terms rather than fabricating a generic example unprompted.
