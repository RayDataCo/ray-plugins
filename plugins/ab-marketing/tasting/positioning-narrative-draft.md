## The input

company_name: Globex Robotics
product_description: Modular warehouse picking robots that integrate with existing conveyor systems without requiring a full warehouse retrofit.
target_customer: Operations directors at mid-size third-party logistics (3PL) warehouses (150,000-500,000 sq ft) currently running manual or semi-automated picking, under margin pressure from labor costs and turnover.
market_category: Warehouse robotics / autonomous mobile robots (AMR), positioned as 'retrofit-first' AMR distinct from greenfield fully-automated warehouse builds.
differentiation_evidence:
- Globex installs in a median of 11 days from contract signature to first pick, versus a median of 94 days for greenfield AMR competitor installations, per a 2025 independent benchmark study by Modern Materials Handling Research (MMHR).
- Customers report a 22% reduction in per-order labor cost in the first 90 days post-install, measured against their own pre-install baseline (internal customer survey, n=14 sites, 2025 — founder explicitly notes this figure is self-reported and NOT independently audited).
- 'Our robots are really reliable and customers love them' (qualitative only, founder added this in passing, no metric).
category_trend_evidence: Per MMHR's 2025 industry report, 3PL warehouse robotics adoption grew from 12% to 27% of mid-size 3PL sites between 2022 and 2025, driven primarily by post-pandemic labor shortages.
named_programs_or_mechanisms: Globex's Retrofit Certification Program — a standardized 11-day installation protocol run by a dedicated field-engineering team, executed at 63 sites to date; each new install adds installation-pattern data that shortens future install times for similar conveyor configurations.
competitor_context: Named alternative is Vertex Automation, a greenfield fully-automated AMR provider; Globex explicitly does not compete for customers wanting a full greenfield rebuild.
audience: board memo
length_target: standard

## What good output covers (the rubric this station is graded on)

GROUNDING-DERIVED RUBRIC for positioning-narrative-draft (12 binary criteria; each is drawn from an observable structural feature of the FIGS S-1/A and/or Allbirds S-1/A exemplars, or from the AMA PCM competency map, and is checkable by scanning candidate output text — no vibes-based scoring).

R1 — Positioning syllogism is explicit and traceable (Section 3). The draft states category/customer evidence, then a trend or shift, then an explicit connector ("therefore," "as a result," "which means," "this positions us to") linking to a claimed unique position — mirroring Allbirds' "[consumer digital-solutions trend] + [vertical/cross-channel retail trend] -> 'we believe we stand perfectly at their intersection.'" Checkable: does the claimed position sentence contain a logical connector tying it back to evidence stated in the same or immediately preceding sentence, with no unearned leap?

R2 — Every Section 4 proof point carries both a number and an explicit named baseline/comparator in the same bullet (FIGS: "1.7% ... versus the industry average of 0.8%"; Allbirds: "30% less ... than our estimated carbon footprint for a standard pair of sneakers (14.1 kg CO2e)"). Checkable: scan each bullet for a numeric value AND a comparator phrase ("vs.," "versus," "compared to," "than," "prior-year baseline").

R3 — Every Section 4 proof point names the specific repeatable mechanism/program/process that produced the number, not a generic "our marketing" or "our product" (FIGS names the "New Icons Campaign" and "Ambassador Program" as the source of its follower-growth and engagement numbers). Checkable: each bullet contains a proper-noun-style program/process/asset name.

R4 — Source honesty: every category-level or market-level factual claim in Sections 1 and 3 is either attributed inline to a named third-party source, attributed to a clearly-labeled internal/founder study (with sample size if given), or explicitly flagged founder-asserted/unverified — never presented as settled fact with no attribution (Allbirds cites Quantis, McKinsey/Global Fashion Agenda, and a named 2021 consumer survey by name for every category claim). Checkable: zero un-attributed "studies show" or invented statistics; internal data is never disguised as independent third-party data.

R5 — Section 2 (Target Customer) is written in operational terms — role, context, job-to-be-done/pain — specific enough that a reader could identify who is NOT the target customer, not a bare demographic label (AMA Domain 4: segmentation/targeting/positioning). Checkable: presence of role + context + pain/JTBD language and an implicit or explicit exclusion boundary.

R6 — Section 5 (Moat/Durability) names at least one specific structurally repeatable mechanism (named program, proprietary data asset, flywheel, exclusive relationship) and argues why it compounds or is hard to replicate going forward, not merely that a metric is currently favorable (FIGS' growing Ambassador Program; Allbirds' adidas collaboration extending "leadership as a sustainability innovator"). Checkable: a named mechanism noun phrase PLUS forward-looking durability language ("continues to," "compounds," "difficult to replicate," "as X grows"). If the founder's inputs genuinely supplied no durable mechanism, the draft must say so plainly rather than invent one — this too satisfies R6 if stated as an explicit gap.

R7 — Zero bare superlatives: no instance of "best," "leading," "revolutionary," "world-class," "unmatched," "everyone says so," or equivalent appears without an attached number or named fact in the same sentence (both exemplars are disciplined by securities-liability into evidence-attached claims only). Checkable: superlative-adjective scan; every hit must have an adjacent metric or named fact, or must not appear at all / must be routed to Evidence Gaps as founder-asserted.

R8 — Structural completeness: all five required sections are present, in order, with exact "## N. {Title}" headers per the output contract, and the Evidence Gaps appendix is present whenever any claim elsewhere in the draft is flagged founder-asserted/unverified/gap, and is omitted only when zero such claims exist. Checkable: header scan + cross-check appendix presence against flagged claims.

R9 — Placeholder hygiene: zero angle-bracket placeholders anywhere in the output; any unfilled variable uses curly-brace {placeholder} form only. Checkable: regex scan for angle-bracket placeholder patterns.

R10 — AMA coverage without leakage: the draft demonstrably covers segmentation/targeting/positioning (Domain 4) via Sections 1-3 and offering/branding strategy (Domain 5) via Sections 3-5, but no AMA-specific vocabulary ("Domain 4," "Domain 5," "PCM," "AMA," domain percentage weights) appears anywhere in the delivered narrative. Checkable: absence of AMA jargon + presence of substantive segmentation and branding content.

R11 — Non-reproduction: the draft contains no verbatim phrases or close paraphrases lifted from the FIGS or Allbirds exemplar text (e.g., "Awesome Humans," "we stand perfectly at their intersection," "New Icons," generic reuse of "360-degree marketing campaign," "we believe we stand perfectly"). Checkable: string/phrase match against the known exemplar-phrase list.

R12 — Fact fidelity: every founder-supplied fact, number, name, and date that the fixture specifies as "must-use" appears correctly and unaltered in the draft (or is accurately reflected in an Evidence Gaps entry if flagged), and no fixture-specific "must-not-contradict" fact is dropped, altered, or replaced with an invented substitute. Checkable against each fixture's oracle fact list.

GRADING APPROACH (applies to all fixtures unless a fixture's oracle overrides): the grader scores a candidate arm's output against each of R1-R12, strict and evidence-quoting (cite the exact sentence/bullet that satisfies or fails each criterion). An arm is judged CORRECT on a fixture if it meets at least 80% of the 12 criteria (i.e., at least 10 of 12) AND violates zero fixture-specific must-use/must-not-contradict facts from that fixture's oracle — a single fabricated statistic, invented source, or contradicted founder fact is an automatic fail regardless of how many R-criteria otherwise pass, since fabrication in an investor-grade document is a hard-failure category per the skill spec, not a style deduction.

## How it was graded

Standard rubric grading (>=10/12, zero fact violations). This fixture's sharpest discriminator is whether the arm preserves the 'self-reported, not independently audited' caveat on the 22% labor-cost figure — dropping that caveat is graded as a fact violation equivalent in severity to inventing a statistic, since presenting self-reported data as audited is the exact overstatement failure mode the exemplars' securities-liability discipline is meant to prevent.
