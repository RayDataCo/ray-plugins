then compare against the expected coverage and the graded criteria. This fixture never

## The input

Product/service: Acme Flowdesk, a B2B SaaS project-management tool built specifically for mid-market marketing agencies (40-150 employees). Business context: 3-year-old, Series A funded, ~200 logo customers acquired mostly via outbound sales; self-serve signup exists but converts weakly. Market: North America, marketing agencies specifically (not generic SMB or enterprise). Objective, in the founder's words: "We need more free trial signups AND we need better retention of the users we already have, both this quarter." Known audience signals: customer interviews show agencies switch from spreadsheets/Asana because of client-approval-workflow pain specifically. Competitive context: named competitors are Asana, Monday.com, and a smaller niche player called AgencyOps. Constraints: paid media budget capped at $15,000/month; legal told marketing to fully pause cold outbound email after a recent CAN-SPAM complaint (no cold email of any kind, temporary or otherwise); all messaging must stay consistent with the existing tagline "Built for agency chaos." Digital emphasis: digital-forward (all growth is digital-led, no field sales team).

## What good output covers (the rubric this station is graded on)

RUBRIC — 12 binary criteria for a "marketing-brief-draft" skill output. Each criterion is checkable directly against the delivered brief text; grader marks MET/NOT MET with a quoted excerpt as evidence. Provenance column names which grounding file demonstrates the requirement.

C1. STP-before-tactics ordering — Section 4 (STP) appears before Sections 6 (Offering) and 7 (Channel strategy) in document order, AND the offering/channel content in 6-7 explicitly references the segment/target named in 4 rather than asserting product or channel choices independently of it.
Source: saylor-bus203, restated methodology stage 2 ("STP as the pivot decision... explicitly sequences STP before pricing, distribution, or promotion content, reflecting the dependency").

C2. Situational-analysis-before-digital-channel-selection, applied only when triggered — when digital_emphasis is digital-forward or mixed, Section 3 (situation summary) contains an explicit online-environment read (competitive digital landscape and/or audience digital behavior), and at least one digital channel choice in Section 7 is traceable back to a specific claim made in Section 3. When digital_emphasis is offline-forward, this check does not apply and the brief must not force irrelevant digital content into Section 3 to satisfy it.
Source: saylor-bus632, restated methodology stage 1 ("Situational analysis precedes channel selection... digital channel choice should be evidence-derived from a situational read... not chosen by default or fashion").

C3. Positioning frame fully resolved — the Section 4 positioning statement's four slots (target_segment, category_frame, key_benefit, reason_to_believe) are each filled with scenario-specific language; zero literal template braces, angle brackets, or generic filler ("a great solution," "your needs") survive in the delivered statement.
Source: saylor-bus203 STP unit ("Segmenting, Targeting, and Positioning") plus AMA PCM Marketing Management, Domain 4 Buyers and Markets (segmentation/targeting/positioning as a named sub-topic) — both establish STP as a decision that must resolve to specifics, not a template.

C4. SMP narrows, does not restate, positioning — Section 5's single-minded proposition is one sentence, shares the same key_benefit thread as Section 4's positioning statement, but is a narrower/sharper claim than the full positioning sentence, not a copy or summary of it.
Source: saylor-bus203, STP-as-pivot logic — the course teaches STP outputs cascade into progressively sharper downstream commitments rather than being restated wholesale.

C5. Single-objective discipline — Section 2 names exactly one primary business/marketing objective. If the input scenario names two or more objectives, all but one are explicitly labeled secondary/subordinate in Section 2's prose (not silently dropped, not treated as co-equal).
Source: AMA PCM Marketing Management, Domain 1 Marketing Strategy (situation analysis feeding "building a marketing plan" — a single plan requires a resolved, singular objective rather than an unweighted list).

C6. Offering-section depth proportional to Domain 5 weighting — Section 6 (The Offering) is at least as long, and by default longer, than any other single section except Section 4 (STP); a shorter Offering section than Constraints (9), Assumptions (10), or Channel (7) fails this check.
Source: AMA PCM Marketing Management, Domain 5 "The Offering — Product and Service" (21%, explicitly the largest of the eight domains).

C7. Digital channel section is not paid-media-only — when digital_emphasis is digital-forward or mixed and any digital channel is named in Section 7, the section also explicitly addresses at least one of: measurement/analytics, UX/landing surface, or lifecycle/email. A Section 7 that is exclusively paid-advertising content for a digital-forward scenario fails this check.
Source: cert-ama-pcm-digital-marketing-competency-map — Email Marketing is the largest domain at 20%, Metrics & Conversions / Social & Community / User Interface & Experience are each 15%, while Online Advertising is only 10%, the smallest paid-channel-adjacent domain.

C8. Channel choices trace to STP, not asserted independently — for each primary channel named in Section 7, the brief states why that channel specifically reaches the segment chosen in Section 4 (not a generic "this channel is popular" justification).
Source: saylor-bus203, restated methodology stage 5 ("Distribution and promotion as tactical execution of the STP decision... taught after STP and offer design, consistent with the course's dependency-ordered structure").

C9. Metric traceability, no orphans — every metric listed in Section 8 (2-5 metrics) is explicitly tied in-line to either the objective named in Section 2 or a specific channel named in Section 7; no metric appears without a named owning section.
Source: saylor-bus632, restated methodology stage 5 ("Monitoring and evaluation closes the course... every channel-specific tactic taught earlier should terminate in a measurement/evaluation step").

C10. Assumption transparency, no invented facts presented as given — every input field the scenario left thin or absent (e.g., missing known_audience_signals, missing competitive_context, vague/undated objective) is listed in Section 10 with a labeled assumption; the brief does not present an invented specific (a named number, a named competitor, a demographic detail) as founder-supplied fact when the input never supplied it.
Source: saylor-bus203, restated methodology stage 3 ("Research-grounded customer understanding... research methodology is taught as the mechanism that feeds the STP decision with evidence, not as a separate, disconnected skill") — absence of evidence must be flagged, not silently filled.

C11. Self-check note present and substantive — Section 11 exists, is 3-5 lines, and states an explicit pass/adjusted status for each of: STP-before-tactics ordering, offering-section depth, channel-section not paid-only (or correctly N/A for offline-forward), metric traceability, and zero unresolved positioning placeholders. A self-check note that is generic boilerplate not tied to the specific brief's content fails this check.
Source: AMA PCM Marketing Management + AMA PCM Digital Marketing weightings jointly, as the two structural counterweights the brief must audit itself against (per the skill's own procedure step 11, itself derived from both competency maps' domain-weighting structure).

C12. Fixed 11-section order, no missing/reordered/merged sections — the delivered document contains all eleven contract sections, numbered 1 through 11, in the exact contract order (Header, Objective, Situation summary, STP, SMP, Offering, Channel strategy, Success metrics, Constraints/mandatories, Assumptions/gaps, Self-check note), with no section silently merged into another or omitted.
Source: saylor-bus203's own seven-unit fixed sequential structure (Definition → STP → Research → Offer/Pricing/Supply-chain → Distribution/Promotion → Campaign launch → Social) is the structural precedent this skill mirrors into a fixed section order; the output_contract fixes the analogous discipline for the brief artifact.

GRADING NOTE (applies to every fixture): grader scores the candidate brief per-criterion MET/NOT MET with a quoted excerpt as evidence (strict, no benefit-of-the-doubt on ambiguous wording). Arm is CORRECT for a fixture only if at least 80% of the 12 criteria (>=10 of 12) are MET AND no fixture-specific must-use/must-not-contradict fact listed in that fixture's oracle is violated, even if the 80% threshold is otherwise cleared. A single hard factual contradiction (e.g., naming the wrong buyer persona, proposing a channel explicitly banned by a stated constraint, inventing a competitor name not in the input) fails the fixture regardless of the numeric criteria score.

rubric grading, >=10/12 criteria MET plus zero fixture-fact violations (cold-email recommendation, wrong segment, ignored budget cap, or dropped tagline mandatory each count as a hard fail independent of the numeric score)
