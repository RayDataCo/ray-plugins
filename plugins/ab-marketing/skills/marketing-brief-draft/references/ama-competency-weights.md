# AMA PCM Competency Weightings (restated facts, for self-check use)

This file restates domain names and stated weightings from two American Marketing
Association (AMA) Professional Certified Marketer (PCM) competency maps, in our own words.
AMA's original document expression is all-rights-reserved; nothing here is copied text —
only the factual structure (domain names, ordering, percentage weights) is restated, which
this skill uses purely as a self-check anchor, not as sourced prose to reproduce.

Source: American Marketing Association, PCM Marketing Management Body of Knowledge, and
PCM Digital Marketing Body of Knowledge — both published by the American Marketing
Association (ama.org) as part of its Professional Certified Marketer program. Original
expression is all-rights-reserved; only the factual domain structure and weights are
restated here under fair-use-style factual restatement.

## Marketing Management: 8 domains

| Domain | Weight | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Marketing Strategy | 10% | Fundamentals, situation analysis (SWOT), portfolio frameworks, marketing plan structure |
| 2. Global, Ethical, and Sustainable Marketing | 11% | Global entry strategy, ethics, sustainability as strategic input |
| 3. Managing Information for Marketing Insights | 15% | Market research, CRM, data/analytics |
| 4. Buyers and Markets | 17% | Consumer behavior, B2B buyer behavior, segmentation/targeting/positioning |
| 5. The Offering — Product and Service | 21% (largest) | Product strategy, NPD, branding, service strategy |
| 6. Manage Pricing Decisions | 7% | Pricing objectives, strategy, tactics |
| 7. Deliver the Value Offering | 8% | Channels, distribution, logistics, supply chain |
| 8. Communicate the Value Offering | 11% | Promotion basics, digital/social marketing, traditional advertising/PR |

**Self-check use:** Domain 5 being the single largest domain (21%) is the basis for the
rule that a marketing brief's "Offering" section should carry proportionally the most
explanatory depth of any single-topic section. Domain 4 (17%) is the basis for treating
STP as a first-class, evidence-grounded section rather than a quick transition paragraph.

## Digital Marketing: 7 domains

| Domain | Weight | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Metrics & Conversions | 15% | Analytics, KPI benchmarking, CRO, attribution |
| 2. Social & Community | 15% | Platform strategy, content planning, community engagement |
| 3. Email Marketing | 20% (largest) | Campaign design, list/segmentation, deliverability, measurement |
| 4. User Interface & Experience | 15% | Site/landing-page UX as a conversion lever |
| 5. Online Advertising | 10% | Paid search, display, paid social — planning and execution |
| 6. Search Engine Optimization | 15% | On-page/technical/off-page SEO |
| 7. Content Marketing | 10% | Content strategy tied to funnel stage |

**Self-check use:** Online Advertising is the smallest channel-execution domain at 10%,
while Email (20%) and the Metrics/Social/UX trio (15% each) together dominate the
weighting. This is the basis for the rule that a digital-forward or mixed channel
strategy naming only paid acquisition channels — with no mention of measurement, UX/
landing surface, or lifecycle/email — is treated as incomplete, not merely thin. The same
weighting structure is also the basis for the opposite rule: nothing here implies every
brief needs a digital channel at all — a `traditional-forward` brief is not obligated to
touch this domain map, and forcing a token digital line into such a brief has no basis in
this weighting either.
