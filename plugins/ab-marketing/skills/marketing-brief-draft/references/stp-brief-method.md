## The pipeline this skill's procedure follows

BUS203 sequences a marketing plan as, in order: definitional grounding; STP as the pivot
decision; research-grounded customer understanding feeding STP; offer/pricing/supply chain
as one linked decision; distribution and promotion as tactical execution of STP; campaign
launch as synthesis; and social media as a distinct modern capstone.

The two principles this skill enforces directly, restated from that sequence:

1. **STP is sequenced before everything tactical.** You cannot credibly choose a channel,
   set a price frame, or write a positioning claim before deciding who you're targeting.
   A brief that names channels or benefits before finishing STP has the dependency
   backwards.
2. **Research feeds STP; it isn't a disconnected section.** Situation-analysis facts
   (market, competitive context, audience signals) exist to be used as evidence inside the
   targeting rationale and positioning statement — not as scene-setting that the rest of
   the brief ignores.

## The digital-execution layer

BUS632 assumes STP as prerequisite and adds channel-execution method on top, restated:

1. **Situational analysis precedes channel selection.** Digital channel choice should be
   evidence-derived from a situational read of the competitive and audience-behavior
   landscape, not chosen by default or platform fashion.
2. **The digital mix is one coherent bundle, not a menu.** Channel strategy should read as
   a single strategic decision analogous to the classic marketing mix, not a list of
   unrelated tactics bolted together.
3. **UX/landing surface is a marketing deliverable, not an engineering handoff.** A
   campaign's conversion surface is in scope for the brief's channel thinking, not
   something to wave at another team with no comment.
4. **Each digital channel is its own discipline** (content/virality, social, search,
   video, email) — a brief naming a digital channel should show channel-specific
   reasoning, not a generic "we'll do social" gesture.
5. **Monitoring and evaluation closes the loop.** Every tactic named in a brief should
   terminate in a measurement step — this is the direct basis for the rule that no metric
   in a brief's success-metrics section may be an orphan with no named objective or
   channel it measures.

## How this skill applies the method

- STP (brief Section 4) is drafted before Offering (Section 6) and Channel Strategy
  (Section 7), and both later sections must trace back to the targeting decision made in
  Section 4 rather than asserting claims independently.
- Situation Summary (Section 3) is written to be citable — later sections should be able
  to point back to a specific bullet, mirroring the "research feeds STP" principle.
- Channel Strategy (Section 7) is checked against the "coherent bundle" and "UX/measurement
  are marketing work" principles: a digital-forward or mixed brief must touch at least one
  of measurement, UX/landing surface, or lifecycle/email alongside any acquisition channel
  named — but a `traditional-forward` brief carries no such obligation, and "coherent
  bundle" cuts both ways: a channel only belongs in the bundle if the targeting decision
  earns it, not because a digital line makes the mix look more modern.
- Success Metrics (Section 8) is checked against the "monitoring closes the loop"
  principle: every metric must name the objective or channel it measures.
