# Gold Structure Checklist

Self-check reference for `positioning-narrative-draft`. Run every section against its
row before returning the document. This checklist restates structural qualities
observed in public-record S-1/A "Business"/"Marketing Strategy" narratives and the AMA
PCM Marketing Management competency map — it does not quote or reproduce those
documents' language, only the pattern.

## Why these two source classes anchor the checklist

- **S-1/A Business-section narratives** (positioning and marketing-strategy
  disclosures, e.g. Allbirds, Inc. Form S-1/A Amendment No. 4, 2021, and FIGS, Inc. Form
  S-1/A Amendment No. 3, 2021) are drafted under securities-liability discipline: a
  materially false or misleading statement carries legal exposure, so the surviving
  language is unusually well-evidenced compared to ordinary marketing copy. That's the
  discipline this checklist imports — every claim earns its place with a number, a
  comparator, or a named source.
- **AMA PCM Marketing Management Body of Knowledge** weights an 8-domain marketing
  curriculum. The two heaviest domains are Domain 5 (The Offering — product, branding,
  service strategy, 21%) and Domain 4 (Buyers and Markets — segmentation, targeting,
  positioning, 17%). Together they're 38% of the model's weighting — more than any
  other pairing. That maps directly onto why Target Customer (Section 2) and
  Differentiated Value (Section 3) get the tightest gates in this skill: they are where
  a competency-graded marketing document earns or loses the most credit.

**Honest provenance, not blanket distillation.** Most of the row-level gates below are
a direct structural echo of the two source classes above — the metric-vs-baseline and
named-mechanism checks on Proof Points, for example, map one-for-one onto patterns
visible in both S-1/A exemplars, and the attribution check on Category Definition
mirrors how both filings cite third-party research rather than asserting category
facts in their own voice. Two specific checks below do not come from either source in
the same direct way: the Target Customer **specificity test** and the Moat/Durability
**multi-year compounding test**. Neither S-1/A exemplar contains a standalone
target-customer or moat/durability narrative to pattern-match against, and the AMA map
supplies domain-level topic weighting, not check-level criteria. Those two checks are
this skill's own applied editorial judgment — a reasonable extension of the same
evidence-first discipline into sections the source material doesn't model explicitly.
They are marked below and held to the same rigor as every other gate; they just aren't
sourced distillation, and the skill doesn't claim otherwise.

## Gate checklist by section

### 1. Category Definition
- [ ] Contains at least one category-level claim that is either sourced (named
      report, dataset, or third party) or explicitly flagged as founder-asserted.
- [ ] If a leadership claim is made ("leading," "first," "#1"), a specific named
      comparator is stated alongside it.
- [ ] If the redefinition/convergence move is used, both evidence legs (the category
      data point and the trend) are independently stated before the claimed position,
      not asserted as a single unsupported leap.

### 2. Target Customer
- [ ] Customer is described by role/context/job-to-be-done, not a demographic label
      alone.
- [ ] *(applied editorial judgment, not sourced distillation — see provenance note
      above)* A reader could name a plausible non-customer directly from the
      description (specificity test).
- [ ] No segment so broad it could describe "anyone who buys things in this category."

### 3. Differentiated Value
- [ ] Written as 1–2 paragraphs of prose, not a bulleted feature list.
- [ ] The syllogism is explicit: evidence/trend stated, then the claimed position
      stated as its consequence — the reader can point to the exact sentence the
      "therefore" depends on.
- [ ] No leap: the claimed position doesn't require a fact that isn't stated somewhere
      in Sections 1–3.

### 4. Proof Points
- [ ] 3–6 bullets/short paragraphs.
- [ ] Every single one contains a number AND a named baseline/comparator
      ({metric} vs {baseline}).
- [ ] Every one names the mechanism (program, process, asset) that produced the
      number — not just the outcome in isolation.
- [ ] Any candidate claim that fails the number+comparator test has been quantified
      with a founder-approved figure, relabeled `{founder-asserted, unverified}`, or
      moved to the Evidence Gaps appendix — never left dressed as evidenced.

### 5. Moat / Durability
- [ ] At least one named, structurally repeatable mechanism (recurring program,
      proprietary asset, data flywheel, network effect, exclusive relationship).
- [ ] *(applied editorial judgment, not sourced distillation — see provenance note
      above)* Explains why the mechanism compounds or resists replication over a
      multi-year horizon — answers "why does this stay true in 3 years," not just
      "what's true today."
- [ ] Not just a restated favorable metric with no mechanism attached.

### Appendix: Evidence Gaps
- [ ] Present if and only if at least one claim anywhere in the draft was thin,
      unsourced, or founder-asserted-only.
- [ ] Each entry names: the section, the specific claim, and exactly what's missing
      (a number / a comparator / a source / a verified mechanism).
- [ ] Omitted entirely when the self-check finds zero gaps — don't include an empty or
      pro-forma appendix.

## Cross-cutting checks (apply to the whole document)

- [ ] No angle-bracket placeholders anywhere — curly-brace `{placeholder}` form only.
- [ ] No unearned superlatives (adjectives like "revolutionary," "world-class,"
      "best-in-class," "cutting-edge") standing alone without an attached number and
      comparator.
- [ ] Section headers are exactly `## 1. Category Definition`, `## 2. Target
      Customer`, `## 3. Differentiated Value`, `## 4. Proof Points`, `## 5. Moat /
      Durability`, and (conditionally) `## Appendix: Evidence Gaps`, in that order.
- [ ] Register is declarative and evidence-first throughout — no hype-adjective
      sentences that don't resolve to a fact.
