# Intake Questions

Per-section prompts for gathering founder inputs before drafting. Use only the
questions for sections not already covered by material the founder has supplied
unprompted (a deck, notes, a memo). Skip re-asking anything already answered.

The goal of intake is to collect real facts, real numbers, and real sources — not to
extract polished marketing language. Rough founder language ("we're basically the only
ones who do X for Y") is fine input; the skill's drafting step is what turns rough
input into evidence-disciplined prose. What intake must not accept as final is a claim
with no number and no comparator behind it if a proof point or moat is being built on
top of it — flag that gap back to the founder at intake time rather than discovering it
only at the Step 3 self-check.

## Category Definition
- What category does this compete in, in the customer's own words for what they'd
  search for or compare it against?
- Is there a market-size, market-growth, or category-trend data point you can point to
  (a report, a public dataset, an internal estimate you're comfortable citing)? Name
  the source.
- Is there a specific trend or shift this company is positioned at the intersection
  of? What's the second data point that supports that trend claim (distinct from the
  category-size data point above)?
- If a leadership or "first" claim is in play — first/leading/largest compared to
  *whom, specifically*? Name the comparator.

## Target Customer
- Who buys or uses this, described by their role and the situation they're in when the
  need arises — not a demographic bucket?
- What job is the customer hiring this to do? What were they doing before (status quo
  alternative) that this replaces or beats?
- Who would clearly NOT be the target customer, even though they're adjacent? (If this
  question is hard to answer, the segment as currently described is still too broad.)

## Differentiated Value
- In plain language: what's the one or two-sentence reason this company wins for that
  customer, in that category, right now?
- What evidence or trend (already surfaced in Category Definition / Target Customer)
  does that reason depend on? Can you point to the specific fact that makes it true,
  rather than just asserting it?
- Is there a leap in the current framing — a claim that sounds true but doesn't
  obviously follow from anything stated? Name what's missing to close that gap.

## Proof Points
- List anything you'd currently call a proof point (a metric, a result, a comparison
  to a competitor or industry baseline).
- For each one: what's the actual number? What's it being compared against (a prior
  period, an industry average, a named competitor, a stated baseline)? What program,
  process, or asset produced that number (a named campaign, a named data system, a
  named partnership)?
- Any claim on this list that's missing a number, a comparator, or a named mechanism —
  flag it now: do you have the missing piece, or should this move to Evidence Gaps /
  get labeled unverified?

## Moat / Durability
- What's structurally hard for a competitor to copy even if they wanted to — a
  proprietary dataset, an exclusive relationship, a network effect, a recurring
  program that compounds over time, a cost or distribution advantage?
- Why does that advantage get *harder* to close over time rather than easier? What's
  the actual mechanism (not just "we're ahead today")?
- If nothing clearly compounds yet, say so directly — a moat section built on a
  today-only metric with no compounding mechanism will fail its gate and needs to be
  either narrowed to what's real or flagged as a gap.

## General thin-input signal

If, after asking, the founder's answer to any of the above is still just an adjective
or a confident assertion with no number/source/mechanism attached, do not smooth it
into polished prose that reads as evidenced. Either ask one more specific follow-up
(what's the number, who's the comparator, what's the source) or route the claim to the
Evidence Gaps appendix / `{founder-asserted, unverified}` label per the main procedure.
