# GAO Effectiveness-Narrative Architecture — Distilled Reference

This file distills the structural pattern this skill is built on. It draws on
two public-domain GAO reports (federal works, not subject to copyright under 17
U.S.C. § 105) and applies to any marketing/communications effectiveness
assessment, not just government campaigns.

## 1. The findings-evidence-recommendation architecture

GAO reports (and this skill's output) follow a fixed shape:

1. State the pressure that made the review necessary (a mandate, a shortfall, a
   funding decision) — not a sales pitch for why the topic matters, just the
   actual triggering question.
2. State the scope: exactly what question(s) the report answers, in measurable
   terms wherever the underlying objective allows it.
3. Describe method: what was reviewed, who evaluated it, how independent that
   evaluation was, what standard or benchmark results were judged against, and
   when the evaluation happened relative to the activity being evaluated.
4. State findings as claim-plus-evidence pairs, numbered, each traceable to a
   specific piece of supplied evidence.
5. State limitations as specific, named constraints — never generic filler.
6. State recommendations, each explicitly derived from a numbered finding.

GAO-25-106719 (military recruiting digital marketing, published Nov 2024) is
the clearest full instance of this shape: it names the recruiting-shortfall
pressure up front, states four scoped questions, describes its method
("reviewed DOD and military service studies, guidance, and plans; interviewed
agency officials; and assessed military services' efforts against commercial
best practices for assessing advertising effectiveness"), reports findings by
category (risk management, strategic goals, funding) with the evidence for
each, and closes with eight recommendations DOD could accept or contest
individually.

## 2. The activity-vs-outcome gap (the core failure mode this skill guards
against)

GAO-25-106719's central finding was not about creative quality — it was that
social media engagement "did not consistently correlate with successful
recruiter contact," and that some strategic goals (e.g., "building a connection
to the STEM field") had no attached target or performance measure at all. GAO's
own language: "Without identifying how such progress will be measured ... it
will be difficult for these services to assess their progress." An un-targeted
goal is not merely under-specified — it is unassessable, and GAO treats that as
a findable defect in its own right rather than something to rewrite on the
evaluee's behalf. This skill applies the identical rule in Section 2 of its
output: it flags unmeasurable objectives rather than quietly converting them
into measurable-sounding language.

The complementary failure mode is illustrated by GAO-06-818 (the 1998-2004
national youth anti-drug media campaign, evaluated 2006): a large, well-funded,
professionally executed campaign whose own independently commissioned
evaluation (by Westat, assessed by GAO against social-science research
standards) "yielded no evidence of a positive outcome in relation to teen drug
use." The campaign had plenty of activity evidence (media weight, reach,
recall) — what it lacked was outcome evidence, and its own evaluator said so.
GAO reported the null result plainly, and Congress subsequently cut the
campaign's funding, a reduction Senate staff attributed partly to the report.
The lesson this skill encodes: a null or negative result that's present in the
input must be reported, not buried under the activity metrics that happen to
look better.

## 3. The ONDCP six-point campaign development/evaluation structure

From GAO-25-107845 (a GAO-convened panel of 12 independent media-campaign
experts, forum held Feb 2025, report published July 2025). The panel organized
rigorous campaign work into two phases with three requirements each:

**Development**
1. Audience understanding — identify the intended audience(s) and the
   underlying behavioral drivers, not just a demographic label.
2. Content, messengers, and delivery — credible, evidence-grounded messaging,
   trusted messengers, multi-platform distribution.
3. Message testing — test messages with the intended audience before launch
   (focus groups, surveys, interviews), don't launch on assumption.

**Evaluation**
4. Defining outcomes — explicit, stated goals up front. The panel noted that
   measuring "behavioral intent to change" or "progress toward behavior change"
   is often more feasible than measuring sustained behavior change directly —
   a useful reframe when a fully outcome-linked metric genuinely isn't
   available, as long as the reframe is stated honestly rather than presented
   as the original goal.
5. Qualified, independent evaluators — multidisciplinary expertise in research
   methods, communications, and subject matter; independence means reporting
   outside the program's own funding chain, not necessarily full organizational
   separation.
6. Measurement timing and methods — run both process evaluation (ongoing,
   allows mid-course correction) and summative evaluation (post-campaign
   assessment); combine media metrics, audience surveys, existing population
   data, and direct observation rather than relying on one data source.

This six-point structure is what Section 3 (Methodology) of the skill's output
is built from, and it is the basis of the optional Section 7 self-check
appendix.

## 4. The four finding tags, defined

- **OUTCOME-SUPPORTED** — evidence ties directly to the stated measurable
  objective. Example shape: objective was "increase qualified-lead form
  submissions from target-industry visitors by X% within Y weeks"; evidence
  supplied was a lead-tracking report showing that exact metric moved by a
  measured amount over that exact window.
- **ACTIVITY-ONLY** — evidence shows marketing activity or output, not the
  outcome. Example shape: objective was the same lead-generation goal above,
  but the only evidence supplied is impressions and click-through rate — real
  numbers, but they describe what the campaign did, not what it produced
  against the stated objective.
- **NEGATIVE/NULL** — evidence shows no effect or a contrary effect on the
  stated objective. Must always be included if present in the input,
  regardless of how it makes the narrative read. GAO-06-818 is the reference
  case.
- **GAP** — the objective exists but no evidence was supplied against it at
  all. Rendered as a specific, named input-needed flag, never filled with an
  invented plausible number.

## 5. Public-domain sourcing note

GAO reports are U.S. federal government works and are not subject to copyright
protection in the United States (17 U.S.C. § 105); GAO-25-107845 states this
verbatim on its own cover. That's why this reference file can excerpt and
restate their language directly with citation — the same courtesy does not
automatically extend to a user's own proprietary campaign data, which should
never be represented in output beyond what was actually supplied.
