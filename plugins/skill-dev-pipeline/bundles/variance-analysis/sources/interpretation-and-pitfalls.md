# Interpretation & Pitfalls

How a management accountant *reads* variances (not just computes them), and the canonical mistakes the skill must defend against.

## Variances are signals, not verdicts

A variance flags *where to look*, not *who's guilty*. Investigate by:
- **Materiality** — flag only variances large in both absolute dollars AND as a % of the standard base. Small ones are noise.
- **Controllability** — assign each variance to the responsibility center that owns it. Don't put a non-controllable variance (see FOH production-volume) on a controllable owner.
- **Management by exception** — spend attention on the material + controllable few.

## Cross-variance linkage (the "gaming" signatures)

Variances interact; favorable + unfavorable pairs often share one cause:
- **Favorable DM price + unfavorable DM usage** ⇒ bought cheap, low-quality material that wasted in production. The "savings" caused the waste.
- **Favorable DL rate + unfavorable DL efficiency** ⇒ used cheaper, lower-skill labor that took longer.
- **Favorable price + unfavorable everything-downstream** ⇒ a purchasing decision that pushed cost into other centers.
A naive reader rewards the favorable line; a competent reader sees the trade.

## Standard currency

Are the standards *currently attainable* or *ideal/stale*? Variances against ideal standards are chronically unfavorable and uninformative. Note the standard's vintage when interpreting.

## Canonical pitfalls the skill must guard against

1. **AQ purchased vs AQ used** — DM *price* uses AQ **purchased**; DM *quantity* uses AQ **used**. Swapping them is the #1 DM error.
2. **Budgeted vs applied FOH** — spending variance uses *budgeted* FOH; production-volume variance uses *applied* FOH (SH × std rate). Confusing the two corrupts both.
3. **Static vs flexed budget** — efficiency/price compare against the *flexible* budget (flexed to actual output), never the static/master budget.
4. **Production-volume variance treated as controllable** — it's a denominator/capacity artifact. Never rank it by raw dollars against spending variances or hang it on an operations owner.
5. **Sign guessing** — derive F/U from the operating-income effect with one rule, every line; don't decide case-by-case.
6. **Forcing a tie-out when purchases ≠ usage** — when AQ purchased ≠ AQ used, the DM price/quantity split won't reconcile to a single flex-budget variance; state the inventory-timing difference instead.
